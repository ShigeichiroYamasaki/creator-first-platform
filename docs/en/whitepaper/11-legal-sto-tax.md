# 11. Legal, STO and Tax

::: danger Professional review required
This chapter is a design boundary, not legal, tax or investment advice. Japanese law, licences, issuer terms and regulatory interpretations must be checked against the actual service before launch.
:::

## Legal entity and rights

A Japanese corporation is intended to be the contracting and accountable operator. It handles music licences, neighbouring rights, creator agreements, consumer terms, privacy, employment, vendor contracts, accounting, tax and disputes. On-chain records are evidence and automation tools; they do not create legal rights by declaration.

## Payment assets and custody

Use of JPYC or another stable-value token depends on the exact issuer, product, contract, network, transfer and redemption model, and current legal status. An approved asset registry records this evidence and can suspend an asset. The design seeks to avoid unnecessary custody, but relaying gas or coordinating payment may still create regulatory, accounting, security and consumer-protection duties.

MockJPYC on Amoy is not JPYC, money, electronic payment value or a claim against an issuer. Public-testnet distribution must be clearly separated from sales, rewards and production balances.

## STO boundary

Any future STO would be a separately structured corporate financing activity conducted under applicable Japanese securities law with licensed specialists where required. Supporter SBTs, governance credentials and subscriptions must not promise dividends, repayment, capital gains or ownership. A testnet credential is not a security token.

## Tax and accounting

The corporation determines revenue recognition, consumption tax, withholding, creator payments, token valuation, expenses and reserves with professional advisers. Creator allocations and smart-contract balances do not by themselves determine accounting ownership or taxable timing. Reconciliation must connect bank, regulated-provider, chain and subledger evidence.

## Governance and directors

Protocol votes do not release directors, officers, reviewers or implementers from their own duties. Directors may need to delay or refuse a resolution that is unlawful, conflicts with binding rights or threatens solvency. Such intervention must be narrow, reasoned, recorded and subject to review.

## AML/CFT, sanctions and fan allocation

Fan allocation can be abused if coordinated or nominee listener accounts fund a colluding creator and make criminal proceeds appear to be legitimate music remuneration. CFP therefore does not treat fan registration as an open payment rail.

The initial production boundary requires:

- fixed subscription payments and a capped, non-transferable allocation preference;
- risk-based listener identity and wallet binding for value-bearing actions;
- creator or entity verification, beneficial-owner checks and approved payout destinations;
- related-party and self-allocation review;
- delayed monthly settlement rather than immediate onward transfer;
- monitoring for concentrated new accounts, common funding or device links, circular flows, rapid payout-destination changes and sanctions exposure; and
- human review, documented holds, appeal and lawful refund or reporting procedures.

Monitoring rules must combine customer, product, geography and transaction risks and be reviewed for effectiveness. Exact thresholds are not public, but aggregate alerts, false positives, handling time and policy versions may be reported without compromising personal data or controls. A contract term cannot by itself remove payment-services, electronic-payment-instrument, AML/CFT or sanctions obligations; the actual custody, control, instruction, refund and intermediation model requires Japanese regulatory review.

If CFP or a connected provider is a regulated entity subject to suspicious-transaction reporting, responsibilities and escalation routes must be documented. Even where CFP is not itself the reporting entity, its contract with regulated payment providers must define screening, information sharing, urgent contact and evidence-preservation responsibilities.
