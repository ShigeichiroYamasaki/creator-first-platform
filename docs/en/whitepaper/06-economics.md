# 6. Economic Model

## Principles

The economic model must support creator sustainability, user value, transparent rules, fair opportunity, rights-first payment, operational continuity and governed change. It does not promise a fixed per-stream price or investment return.

## Subscription and network execution

A monthly price may include an explicitly described service component for network execution so that users do not need to purchase gas separately for ordinary actions. The corporation charges for the service, accounts for actual costs and bears execution risk; it must not market an unspecified pooled crypto asset or resell gas as an investment product. Legal and tax treatment requires confirmation before launch.

## Allocation structure

After recognised deductions, a governed distributable amount may be divided into:

- a usage-related creator pool;
- a growth and discovery pool; and
- an operations and resilience pool.

The usage pool can combine verified usage with a user-centred component so that part of each subscription reflects that user's listening. Explicit support may influence a separate bounded allocation. Growth support can use transparent criteria and community matching, subject to Sybil resistance and caps.

## Creator floor and corporate sustainability

Governance may define a minimum creator allocation ratio, but it cannot ignore taxes, refunds, rights holds or solvency. The corporation may retain a documented and governed operating margin; “not profit maximising” does not mean operating at an undisclosed loss.

## Measurement and change

Every formula has a version, input commitment, calculation evidence, approval record and effective period. Simulations must publish assumptions. Proposed changes pass legal, economic, security and both-house review before activation.
