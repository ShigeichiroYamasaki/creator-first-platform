# 5. Creator Onboarding

## Target participant

The initial creator is an independent, artist-direct artist who retains control over material production, distribution and marketing decisions. The platform must not equate “independent” with an unverified self-declaration or exclude legitimate use of external professional services.

## Staged onboarding

Onboarding progresses through explicit levels:

1. account created and authenticated;
2. public creator profile established;
3. identity and authority reviewed;
4. works, masters, collaborators and rights evidence registered;
5. distribution agreement and destination address approved; and
6. distribution enabled only after review gates pass.

Each level grants only the minimum capability needed. A profile may exist before rights verification, but unverified content must not receive production distribution.

## Rights and collaborators

For each release, the creator records work identifiers, master ownership, territories, licences, collection mandates, collaborators and proposed splits. Collaborators receive a confirmation or dispute path. Version history is retained; a changed split does not rewrite earlier settlement evidence.

## Fair review

Review criteria should be published and explainable. Automation may flag missing or inconsistent evidence, but adverse decisions require human review and an appeal route. New creators should not be rejected merely because they lack historical popularity.

## Public-testnet demonstration

The demo uses synthetic or explicitly licensed test content and mock attestations. It validates workflow and usability, not legal clearance. Pre-approved experiment participants may receive capped Test POL and MockJPYC after wallet binding; this funding does not establish creator status or production eligibility.
