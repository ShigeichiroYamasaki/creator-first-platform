# 2. Market and Problem

## What streaming improved

Streaming made broad catalogues immediately accessible, reduced transaction friction and created recurring revenue. It is an essential user experience rather than a feature the project intends to discard.

## Structural problems

The project investigates several connected problems:

- users cannot easily see how their payments become creator revenue;
- aggregate pro-rata distribution may reinforce concentration around already popular catalogues;
- ranking and recommendation systems shape the market but are rarely governable by participants;
- rule-making authority and change history are difficult to inspect;
- usage verification conflicts with privacy when raw listening histories are over-collected;
- fraudulent streams and large volumes of synthetic content can distort allocation; and
- creator support is often considered only after a play, instead of through direct, community and early-stage support.

These observations do not imply that every incumbent service behaves identically. Market claims and quantitative assumptions must be sourced, dated and revalidated before external financial decisions.

## Proposed response

CFP combines a convenient music service with transparent rights records, user-centred allocation components, explicit supporter relationships, auditable treasury views, contestable discovery modes and joint governance. It seeks plural paths to discovery instead of one opaque score.

## Market opportunity and limits

The target is an underserved group of artist-direct independent creators and users who value traceability and participation. The project must still prove catalogue quality, licensing feasibility, retention, operating cost, fraud resistance and whether governance adds value without creating excessive friction. Public-testnet activity is evidence gathering, not product-market-fit proof.
